## 1. Core

| Item | Definition |
|---|---|
| Title | *The Early Shelf* (`PROPOSED`) |
| Logline | A 14-year-old spending the last two weeks of summer at her grandmother's mountain railway station finds that the lost-and-found receives things the night before they're lost, and that giving them back early comes with a price |
| Genre | Serialized speculative mystery-adventure |
| Tone | Wonder with an undercurrent of unease. Warm, earnest characters; light humor that never undercuts the stakes; mild peril |
| Audience | 13+, about PG, not made for kids |
| Audience promise | Every episode, something Larkspur hasn't lost yet turns up on the shelf, or the shelf's price comes due, and Wren has to choose what to give back |
| Theme | Giving something back always costs something. Secrets make that cost heavier; sharing it makes it bearable |
| Narration | Wren narrates in past tense, as if telling us about "that summer" |
| What makes it distinctive | (1) A lost-and-found that shows tomorrow. (2) The price is memory, and paid memories become small glowing objects you can hold, hide, and give back. (3) One station, one clock, one train a day: a contained world where every object matters. (4) Every clue is an object or an action, never on-screen text |

## 2. Setting

**Larkspur Junction** is a sleepy station at the end of a mountain branch line, with a village below and a railway
tunnel just past the end of the platform. One train a day, a two-car vintage diesel railcar called "the 6:14",
arrives at 6:14 pm and leaves at 6:20. The rail company is closing the station: **the last 6:14 runs on the last
Sunday of August.**

**Season timeline (canon):**
- **Ep1:** Wren arrives Monday of the second-to-last week of August.
- **Eps 1–4:** that week, Monday to Saturday.
- **Ep5:** opens Saturday night; the week passes; ends at the Saturday midnight before the last train.
- **Ep6:** the last Sunday, ending at midnight.

## 3. World rules (canon; cannot be contradicted)

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| R1 | **Arrival.** At midnight, when the platform clock strikes twelve, every object that will be lost on the station grounds before the next midnight appears on the Early Shelf. Each is an exact copy, including any damage the loss will cause |
| R2 | **Catch-up.** When the owner actually loses the object, the early copy vanishes from the shelf at that moment |
| R3 | **Giving back early.** If the early copy is handed to its owner before the loss, the two copies become one, the loss does not happen, and tomorrow changes |
| R4 | **Another hand.** Picking up your own early copy doesn't count. A thing must be given by someone else's hand, knowingly |
| R5 | **The price.** Whoever gives a thing back early loses one of their own memories. The shelf chooses which; the giver can't choose or know in advance. **The bigger the change to tomorrow, the bigger the memory:** a moment for a small change, something precious for a hurt prevented, a whole person for a life saved |
| R6 | **The tell.** Each early return makes the platform clock jump forward exactly one minute |
| R7 | **The Keeper's Drawer.** Each paid memory becomes a small, faintly glowing object in the brass drawer inside the clock |
| R8 | **Returning a memory.** A memory-object can only be returned to its owner by someone else's hand (R4), and the giver pays a memory of their own (R5). On return, the object dissolves into light and the memory comes back whole |
| R9 | **Range.** The rules only hold within earshot of the clock: the station grounds, including the tunnel mouth. If the clock leaves Larkspur, the shelf and the drawer go with it |
| R10 | **Ordinary returns are free.** Handing back something *after* it was lost (normal lost-and-found work) costs nothing |

**Visual grammar:** an arriving or returning object fades in or out with a soft amber glow. Memory-objects glow
pale gold. These are the only "magic" visuals. No portals, sparkles, or voices.

### Wren Halloway (protagonist)
- **Situation:** sent to Larkspur "for the rest of the summer" while her parents "sort some things out". She doesn't
  know what, and nobody will say.
- **Want:** to be told the truth and trusted with it.
- **Need:** to learn that truth has weight, and that carrying it alone isn't strength.
- **Flaw:** impulsive; breaks promises when she's sure she's right; acts alone.
- **Fear:** being the last to know; being left behind.
- **Signature prop:** her mother's tiny brass pipe-whistle on a thin chain. She plays her mother's four-bar tune on
  it every night.
- **Arc:**
  - *Start:* "Adults keep secrets, so I'll find out myself."
  - *Middle:* she pays the price herself (Ep3) and learns who has been paying all along (Ep4).
  - *End:* she chooses to pay for someone who wronged her family (Ep5–6). She becomes co-keeper under the rule
    "nobody pays alone".
- **Voice:** dry, observant narration; quick, honest dialogue; humor as armor.

### Ida Halloway (keeper)
- **Want:** to keep Larkspur safe and keep Wren out of it.
- **Flaw:** secrecy; martyrdom. She carries everything alone and refuses help.
- **Secrets, in reveal order:**
  1. *(Ep1)* The shelf exists, and she is its keeper.
  2. *(Ep2–3)* Ansel Crane was her apprentice. She says she has never seen him.
  3. *(Ep4)* She has secretly given things back for 30 years, paying with her own memories. Each red-thread entry in
     her ledger is one of those returns, and the losses explain her "forgetfulness".
  4. *(Ep4)* She hid the Keeper's Drawer.
  5. *(Ep5)* She never warned 16-year-old Ansel about the price before he gave his father's lantern back. Her
     secrecy cost him his father.
- **Signature props:** a brass key on a cord (winds the clock and opens the drawer); a spool of red thread in her
  dark green keeper's apron.
- **Arc:**
  - *Start:* "Never give back early. Never tell."
  - *Middle:* her secrets come out, and her refusal to accept help leaves her memories at risk (Ep5).
  - *End (Ep6):* "Not alone, then." She accepts help and hands the key to Wren.
- **Voice:** clipped, wry, tender underneath.
- **Tell:** occasional memory slips; she calls Wren "Nora" once in Ep1.

### Theo Marsh (friend, then apprentice)
- **Want:** for things to make sense and the clock to keep running.
- **Flaw:** needs proof; cautious; watches instead of acting.
- **Fear:** that Larkspur, and he, will be left behind when the station closes.
- **Secret:** he has logged every unexplained one-minute jump of the clock for three years (31 jumps) and has
  quietly suspected Ida.
- **Signature props:** his grandfather's silver pocket watch; a pencil notebook (shown with tally marks only, never
  readable text).
- **Arc:**
  - *Start:* a skeptic who watches.
  - *Ep3:* trusts Wren against the evidence.
  - *Ep4:* pays a memory for her without being asked.
  - *Ep6:* chooses to pay for Ida, and becomes apprentice.
- **Voice:** precise, deadpan, warm once he lets you in.

### Ansel Crane (opposing force)
- **Cover:** a polite, precise rail-heritage assessor who arrives on the 6:14 in Ep2 to catalogue what is "worth
  keeping" before the station closes.
- **Truth:** Ida's apprentice 30 years ago. At 16 the Early Shelf showed his father's signal lantern, meaning his
  father would be lost in a rockslide at the tunnel. Ansel gave the lantern back early. His father lived, and the
  shelf took every memory Ansel had of him. Ida had never told him the price. He left Larkspur on the 6:14 the next
  day, unable to remember why the stranger on the platform was crying.
- **Want:** that memory back.
- **Method:** take the clock to the city, where returns would work (R9) and he could pay strangers to bear the price.
- **Flaw:** resentment; believes the cost should fall on someone else.
- **Arc:**
  - *Ep2–4:* taker and manipulator, but never cruel.
  - *Ep5:* revealed as a grieving son.
  - *Ep6:* given his memory back freely, he becomes a giver. He pays for Ida and keeps the clock in Larkspur.
- **Signature traits:** a white streak in his dark hair (visible in the 30-year-old photo too); charcoal coat; a
  leather case.
- **Voice:** soft, exact, never raised.

### Abe Crane (the man on the bench)
- **Role:** sits on the platform bench for every 6:14 with his old red signal lantern at his feet. He feeds Ledger.
  He is waiting for a son who "left on that train and never came back".
- **Ep2:** half-rises when Ansel steps off the train, then sits back down as Ansel walks past without a glance.
- **Ep5:** speaks once.
- **Ep6:** raises the red lantern with his son.
- **Production note:** recurring background. Build one character reference and reuse it.

## 5. Relationships

- **Wren ↔ Ida:** love strained by secrecy. They move from rules and lies to honesty and shared keeping.
- **Wren ↔ Theo:** a wary first meeting, then an alliance and real friendship. Wren loses the memory of meeting
  him (Ep6), and he is gracious about it.
- **Ida ↔ Ansel:** an old mentor-apprentice wound. She failed to warn him; he blames her. They partly reconcile in
  Ep6.
- **Ansel ↔ Abe:** a son who can't remember his father and a father who never stopped waiting. Reunited in Ep6.
- **Wren ↔ Ansel:** antagonist, then the person she chooses to pay for.

## 6. Central season question

> **What does the Early Shelf take in return for a second chance, and who has been paying?**

**Intended season-level answer (Ep6):**
- The shelf takes a memory from whoever gives a thing back.
- **Ida has paid alone for thirty years**, and Ansel once paid everything.
- The price is meant to be shared. The season ends with Wren, Theo, and Ansel paying together to restore Ida, the
  clock staying in Larkspur, and a new keepers' rule: **"We give back what matters, and nobody pays alone."**

**How the answer builds:**

| Ep | What the episode adds |
|---|---|
| 1 | The shelf is real, and there's a rule |
| 2 | "Larkspur always takes something back"; keepers existed before |
| 3 | Wren pays without knowing the price |
| 4 | The price is memory; Ida has been paying (answers "what" and "who") |
| 5 | Ansel paid everything; carrying it alone harms, and making others pay is wrong |
| 6 | Sharing the price (resolution) |

## 7. Opposing forces and escalation

1. **Ansel Crane, the external force.** His threat escalates each episode:
   - *Ep2:* arrives and catalogues the station.
   - *Ep3:* recommends removing the clock.
   - *Ep4:* finds the drawer.
   - *Ep5:* sets a deadline (the last train) and plans to make strangers pay.
   - *Ep6:* the removal he started is now bigger than him.
2. **The shelf's price, the internal force.** Unknown in Ep1–2, paid unknowingly in Ep3, understood in Ep4, deadly
   to Ida's memories in Ep5–6.
3. **The station closing, the clock-on-the-wall deadline.** Mentioned in Ep2; it becomes the Ep6 climax.

Stakes rise from personal (a whistle) to friend (Theo's safety) to self (Wren's memories) to family (Ida's mind) to
place (the shelf leaving Larkspur forever).

## 8. Change and consistency

**What changes across the season:**
- Wren's knowledge and her memories.
- Trust between Wren and Ida.
- Theo moves from skeptic to apprentice.
- Ansel moves from taker to giver.
- The clock comes down (Ep5) and goes back up (Ep6).
- The tower stair is lost in Ep3, replaced by a ladder.
- The station closes to trains after Ep6.

**What must stay consistent:**
- The world rules R1–R10.
- Character designs and signature props.
- The midnight timing and the 6:14/6:20 times.
- The amber and pale-gold glow grammar.
- No on-screen text.
- Ida's key never leaves its cord except when Wren takes it (Ep4) and when Ida gives it to her (Ep6).
- Wren's whistle is always worn on her chain from Ep2 on.
- The merged pocket watch is uncracked.

## 10. Deliberately open for Season 2 (declared, not abandoned)

- **Nora.** Wren's mother was a small girl in the keepers' photo, and Wren's whistle is a keeper's whistle. Why did
  Nora leave Larkspur? Her car keys arrive on the shelf in the final beat of Ep6.
- **Unreturned memories.** Some of Ida's lesser memories stay in the drawer: the keepers' ongoing work.
- **Wren's own paid memories.** She lost the memory of meeting Theo, and others in Ep6. She is unaware of some of
  them.
- **The closed station.** No more trains, but the lost-and-found stays open.

## 11. Story-side production rules

- No clue may depend on reading text. Ledger, photo, and notebook are read through objects, colors, thread, and
  tally marks. Any unavoidable text is a Remotion overlay, not a generated image.
- Reuse first: the 6:14 arrival and departure, the clock striking midnight, and the shelf-glow effect are built once
  and reused.
- Supporting characters with no lines are shown from behind or at a distance (for example, the umbrella woman in
  Ep1).
- Peril is shown through aftermath and sound (the stair collapse), never as injury.
