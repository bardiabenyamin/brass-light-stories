# Six-Episode Roadmap — *The Early Shelf*, Season 1

Status: **APPROVED CANON 2026-11-09** (owner: "Series approved: The Early Shelf roadmap and Episode 1 concept, as written.") · Prompt 01, Parts C, D, and E
Canon source: `series-bible.md` (rules R1–R10, clues K1–K11). Ledger: `cliffhanger-ledger.md`.

> **Season question:** What does the Early Shelf take in return for a second chance, and who has been paying?

---

## Causal spine (each ending causes the next problem)

| Ep | Ends with… | …which causes the next episode's problem |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wren's own whistle appears on the shelf: she will lose it tomorrow | Ep2: she can't use the shelf to save it (R4, Ida refuses), so she guards it, and it's lost to the stranger who arrives on the 6:14 |
| 2 | Recovering the whistle from Crane leads to a photo proving Ida lied about knowing him | Ep3: Ida's half-truth ("it cost him more than he could pay") plus her promise; Crane's push to remove the clock; then Theo's cracked watch tests that promise |
| 3 | Wren breaks the promise, saves Theo, and loses her mother's song | Ep4: Ida must confess the price and her 30 years of paying; Wren hunts for her song and finds the drawer |
| 4 | Wren acts on Crane's hint and takes Ida's key; Crane follows her up the ladder, finds the drawer, and takes his father-memory | Ep5: he demands it back, Ida refuses, and he sets the deadline: the clock leaves on the last train |
| 5 | Learning that Crane's father waits on the bench, Wren offers to pay for him | Ep6: she pays (and forgets Theo); Crane's removal is still in motion; the race to return Ida's memories before the last 6:14 |
| 6 | Season resolved; declared Season 2 hook: Nora's keys | (Season 2) |

## Episode 1 — "The Early Shelf"

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | On her first night at Larkspur Junction, Wren watches a yellow umbrella appear on her grandmother's lost-and-found shelf, a day before anyone loses it |
| **Wren's immediate goal** | Find out what the bottom shelf is and why Ida guards it |
| **Conflict and stakes** | Ida forbids her to touch it and won't explain. Wren is alone in a strange place with adults keeping secrets, again. Stakes: her trust in Ida, and whether she is imagining things |
| **Key event (direction change)** | The next evening a passenger leaves the identical umbrella (same mended tear, same bird charm) on the platform bench. At that moment the shelf copy vanishes. Theo hands in the real one |
| **Episode question, introduced and resolved** | "Is the shelf really showing tomorrow's lost things?" **Yes.** Confronted, Ida confirms it: the shelf shows what Larkspur will lose, and a keeper watches and **never** gives anything back early. Wren promises |
| **The audience learns** | R1, R2; Ida is a keeper; the rule. Plants: red-thread ledger entries (K1); "clock gained a minute last night" (K2); Ida calls Wren "Nora" (K3); rotten tower stairs (K4); Wren's whistle tune (K5); Ida's key (K6); Abe and Ledger on the bench |
| **Still unresolved** | *Why* never give back early? What do the red threads mean? Why does the clock jump? |
| **Season advance** | Establishes the shelf and the rule whose price is the season question |
| **Cliffhanger** | **Impossible evidence.** Final beat: midnight. Wren watches the clock strike. On the shelf, her mother's brass whistle glows into being, while the real one hangs at her throat. She holds one in each hand. VO: *"Tomorrow, I lose the only thing my mom ever gave me."* |
| **Next opening payoff** | Ep2 opens seconds later: Wren tries to "give it back to herself" and nothing happens (R4). Ida, woken by the chime, takes the early whistle out of her hand: "It has to come from someone else's hand, and don't you dare ask me. Larkspur always takes something back." She latches it behind the grille |

**Beat outline (about 5:00):**
- 0:00–0:10: cold-open hook (the umbrella glows in).
- 0:10–0:45: Wren arrives on the 6:14; Ida, Theo (winding the clock), Abe and Ledger on the bench.
- 0:45–1:30: the office and the rules; the red-thread ledger; dinner in the kitchen, where Ida says "Nora".
- 1:30–2:15: midnight; Wren whistles her tune and peeks; the umbrella appears.
- 2:15–3:15: the next 6:14; the umbrella is lost and the shelf copy vanishes; Theo hands it in, and the details match.
- 3:15–4:15: confrontation; the truth and the promise.
- 4:15–5:00: midnight; the two whistles.

## Episode 2 — "The Keeper's Whistle"

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | Wren knows she'll lose her mother's whistle today, so she guards it all day, until the 6:14 brings a stranger who knows exactly what it is |
| **Wren's immediate goal** | Keep the whistle; then, once it's gone, get it back |
| **Conflict and stakes** | She can't use the shelf (R4; Ida refuses, and she promised). The loss is coming no matter what. Stakes: the only thing she has from her mother |
| **Key event** | Ansel Crane, a rail-heritage assessor, steps off the 6:14. Wren helps lift his case and her chain snaps. At 6:16, in the office, Ida sees the early whistle vanish. **No one hands the whistle in** |
| **Episode question, introduced and resolved** | "Who has Wren's whistle?" **Resolved:** Wren and Theo work it out (Crane was the only one beside her at 6:16). Crane hands it back smoothly: "Found it. Meant to hand it in. A *keeper's* whistle. Haven't seen one in thirty years" |
| **The audience learns** | R4; price hinted ("Larkspur always takes something back"); the station closes after the last 6:14 in under two weeks; Crane knows about keepers; Ida claims she has never seen him (K7 setup). Plants: Abe half-rises as Crane passes and sits back down (K8); Crane lingers at the empty lantern hook (K9) |
| **Still unresolved** | How does Crane know about keepers? Why did Ida lie? |
| **Season advance** | Introduces the opposing force, and the fact that others have kept (and paid for) the shelf before |
| **Cliffhanger** | **Exposure.** Final beat: at night, Wren searches Ida's ledger and finds an old sepia photo. Young Ida in a keeper's apron stands beside a teenage boy with a white streak in his hair (Crane), both wearing brass whistles like Wren's. A little girl stands half out of frame. VO: *"Grandma said she'd never seen him before."* |
| **Next opening payoff** | Ep3 opens at breakfast: Wren lays the photo on the table. Ida admits it: Ansel was her apprentice; he gave things back early; "It cost him more than he could pay," and she sent him away. "Promise me you'll never find out what it costs" |

**Beat outline:**
- 0:00–0:40: payoff (R4 fails; Ida confiscates the early whistle).
- 0:40–1:30: Wren guards the whistle; Theo's skeptical help; news of the closure.
- 1:30–2:30: the 6:14; Crane arrives; Abe's half-rise; the case lift; the chain snaps (insert).
- 2:30–3:15: the early copy vanishes; the search; the deduction.
- 3:15–4:15: Wren confronts Crane and gets the whistle back.
- 4:15–5:00: the photo.

## Episode 3 — "The Cracked Watch"

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | The shelf shows Theo's pocket watch with its glass cracked, and Wren has to decide whether to break her promise to save him |
| **Wren's immediate goal** | Stop Theo from getting hurt tomorrow |
| **Conflict and stakes** | Her promise and Ida's warning of an unnamed cost. Crane's needling: "Ida's rule has let people get hurt." Theo doesn't believe the shelf and plans to climb the rotten stairs to fix the jumping clock. Stakes: Theo's safety against her word and an unknown price |
| **Key event** | At dawn, Wren gives Theo the early watch. In his hand the two watches become one, *uncracked*, and on the platform the clock jumps a minute while Theo watches (R3, R6) |
| **Episode question, introduced and resolved** | "Can Wren change tomorrow and save Theo?" **Yes.** That afternoon the tower stairs collapse with no one on them (K4 payoff) |
| **The audience learns** | R3, R6. Theo's log: 31 unexplained jumps in three years (K2). Crane formally recommends removing the clock to a city museum when the station closes: "Without the clock, Ida, there's no shelf" (R9 hinted). Ida's half-truth about Ansel |
| **Still unresolved** | What did it cost? Who made the 31 jumps? |
| **Season advance** | Wren pays the price for the first time, without knowing it. The season question becomes personal |
| **Cliffhanger** | **Reversal (emotional turn).** Final beat: evening on the platform. Relieved, Wren lifts her mother's whistle to play the tune she has played every night of her life, and can't remember a single note. VO: *"I saved Theo. And something took my mom's song."* |
| **Next opening payoff** | Ep4 opens with Wren telling Ida. Ida goes pale: "You gave something back." Then the truth: whoever gives a thing back early pays with a memory (R5), and Ida has been paying for thirty years |

**Beat outline:**
- 0:00–0:45: the photo and the half-truth; Crane announces the clock removal.
- 0:45–1:45: Theo's log; his plan to go up the tower.
- 1:45–2:30: midnight; the cracked watch appears.
- 2:30–3:30: the dilemma; the dawn return; the merge and the clock jump.
- 3:30–4:15: the stair collapse (aftermath and sound).
- 4:15–5:00: the lost song.

## Episode 4 — "The Keeper's Drawer"

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | The shelf takes memories as payment, so Wren goes looking for where it keeps them |
| **Wren's immediate goal** | Get her mother's song back |
| **Conflict and stakes** | Ida says "what's paid is paid" and hides the drawer. Reaching the clock room means taking Ida's key and climbing to where the stairs fell. Crane's hint pulls her on. Stakes: Wren's memory of her mother, and Ida's trust |
| **Key event** | Using Ida's key, Wren and Theo open the Keeper's Drawer inside the clock: dozens of tiny glowing objects (R7), most of them Ida's. A red child's mitten matches the one worn by the girl in the photo (K3) |
| **Episode question, introduced and resolved** | "Where do paid memories go, and can Wren get hers back?" **Resolved:** in the drawer. Wren's own hand can't restore the tin music box. When Theo picks it up and hands it to her ("Here, it's yours"), it dissolves into light and the song comes back (R8). Theo blinks: "I can't remember the first clock I ever fixed" |
| **The audience learns** | R5, including Ida's line "The bigger the change, the bigger the memory it takes"; R7, R8. The red threads match Theo's jump log (K1 and K2 paid off). Ida's "forgetfulness" was the price. Crane: "Lost things always end up somewhere. Even those" (K10) |
| **Still unresolved** | Ida's memories; what Crane really wants |
| **Season advance** | Answers the first half of the season question: what the shelf takes, and that Ida has paid alone |
| **Cliffhanger** | **Arrival.** Final beat: the ladder creaks. Crane climbs into the clock room, sees the open drawer, reaches past them and lifts out a small red tin toy train. Voice breaking: *"Thirty years. She had it all along."* |
| **Next opening payoff** | Ep5 opens on the same moment. Crane tells them what the train is: at 16 he gave his father's signal lantern back early to save him from a rockslide in the tunnel, and the shelf took every memory of his father |

**Beat outline:**
- 0:00–0:45: confession (R5; 30 years; red threads equal the jumps).
- 0:45–1:30: Crane's hint on the platform.
- 1:30–2:45: night; the key; the ladder; the drawer.
- 2:45–3:45: the music box; Theo's accidental gift; the song returns.
- 3:45–4:15: Ida's mitten and the scale of her loss.
- 4:15–5:00: Crane arrives.

## Episode 5 — "The Man on the Bench"

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | Crane will take the clock, and every memory inside it, on the last train, unless someone pays to give him back his father |
| **Wren's immediate goal** | Stop the clock from leaving Larkspur |
| **Conflict and stakes** | Crane asks Ida to give the train back; she refuses, because one more payment "might be the last of Nora". Crane: "Then the clock leaves on Sunday's last train. In the city I'll find someone who will." He has the company's removal order. The clock is taken down and crated (R9 made explicit). Stakes: Ida's memories, the shelf, and strangers made to pay |
| **Key event** | Wren learns that Abe, the old man who waits on the bench for every 6:14, is Crane's father. Abe: *"My boy saved my life in that tunnel. Next morning he looked at me like a stranger and got on the train. He came back on Wednesday's 6:14 and walked right past me. Same as thirty years ago."* (K8, K9) |
| **Episode question, introduced and resolved** | "Who is Crane, really, and why does he need the clock?" **Resolved:** a son who can't remember his father, who needs the clock because returns only work within its earshot (R9) and no one in Larkspur will pay for him |
| **The audience learns** | R9 explicitly. Ida's real guilt: she never warned Ansel about the price. "I should have told him. I didn't know it would take everything" |
| **Still unresolved** | Will anyone pay? Will the clock leave? |
| **Season advance** | Shows the two wrong answers to the price: carrying it alone (Ida) and making others pay (Crane) |
| **Cliffhanger** | **Decision.** Final beat: Saturday midnight, the night before the last train. On the baggage trolley, the crated clock chimes twelve, muffled. Wren walks the length of the platform to Crane and holds out her hand: *"Give me the train. I'll give it back to you."* Ida, behind her: *"Wren, you won't get to choose what it takes."* Wren doesn't turn around |
| **Next opening payoff** | Ep6 opens on the same moment. Crane puts the train in her hand, and she gives it back to him. It dissolves into light; he remembers: *"Abe. His name is Abe."* Wren turns to Theo, puzzled: *"…Who are you?"* |

**Beat outline:**
- 0:00–0:50: Crane's confession; Ida's refusal; the deadline.
- 0:50–1:45: the clock comes down (empty bracket, crate on the trolley).
- 1:45–3:00: Abe on the bench; the revelation.
- 3:00–4:00: Ida's real guilt; Wren decides; the argument ("You never let anyone help you").
- 4:00–5:00: midnight; the offer.

## Episode 6 — "The Last 6:14" (season finale)

| Field | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | On the station's last day, Wren has until the final train leaves to give her grandmother back her memories, and to keep the clock in Larkspur |
| **Wren's immediate goal** | Return Ida's most important memories before the 6:14 departs with the crate, and keep the clock |
| **Conflict and stakes** | Crane can't cancel the removal on paper on a Sunday ("I set this in motion. I can't stop it with paper"). The crew loads the crate at departure. Every return costs the giver. Ida refuses help. Stakes: Ida's mind, the shelf, a father and son |
| **Key event** | Ida accepts help: "…Not alone, then." Wren, Theo, and Ansel return Ida's memories at the crate: Wren gives the red mitten (Nora as a child), Theo the pressed larkspur (Ida's wedding day), Ansel the paper lantern (Ida's memory of baby Wren). As the crate is lifted, Abe arrives with his red lantern. Ansel says "Dad," and together they raise it: the railway stop signal. The train holds, and Ansel orders the crate left on the platform |
| **Episode question, introduced and resolved** | "Can they save Ida's memories and the clock before the last train leaves?" **Yes.** The last 6:14 leaves without the clock |
| **The audience learns** | The full season answer (see the bible, §6). Ida to Ansel: "I should have told you what it cost." Ansel: "You should have. So should I" |
| **Still unresolved (declared for Season 2)** | Nora's past as a keeper; the unreturned memories; Wren's own lost memories |
| **Season advance** | **Resolves the season question.** The price is shared. Ida rehangs the clock with Theo and gives Wren her key: *"Keepers. Plural. We give back what matters, and nobody pays alone."* |
| **Ending** | **Finale resolution, then a declared Season 2 hook (arrival).** Midnight; the rehung clock strikes twelve; Ledger hops onto the Early Shelf (K11). A set of car keys glows into being on an enamel larkspur keyring. Wren knows them. VO: *"Tomorrow, my mom loses her keys. Here."* |
| **Next opening payoff** | Season 2, Episode 1 opens on Nora stepping out of her car at the closed station |

**Beat outline:**
- 0:00–0:50: the payoff (Ansel remembers; Wren forgets Theo; Theo: "You called me 'clock boy' for three days. You can do it again").
- 0:50–2:00: the last day; Ida accepts help.
- 2:00–3:15: the returns at the crate as the 6:14 pulls in.
- 3:15–4:00: father and son; the red lantern; the train holds.
- 4:00–4:40: the clock rehung; the new rule.
- 4:40–5:15: the Season 2 hook.

**Type rotation:**
- Ep1: impossible evidence.
- Ep2: exposure.
- Ep3: reversal (emotional turn).
- Ep4: arrival.
- Ep5: decision.
- Ep6: resolution, then a declared arrival hook.

No type repeats back to back. There are no fake deaths, no dream resets, and no twist without setup.

**References to build once and reuse all season:**
- **Characters:** Wren, Ida, Theo, Crane, Abe, plus the cat (optional).
- **Locations:** 4, each in three lighting states: 6:14 golden hour, interior amber lamp, midnight blue.
- **Props:** the yellow umbrella, the whistle, the pocket watch (cracked and whole), the ledger, Ida's key, the photo,
  the music box, the toy train, the red lantern, the crate and trolley, the car keys, and a memory-objects set.

### Expensive or risky shots and their simplifications

| Shot | Risk | Simplification |
|---|---|---|
| The 6:14 arriving and departing (Ep1, 2, 6) | Vehicle consistency and motion | Generate **once**: a slow arrival at dusk, mostly lights and sound. Reuse in all three episodes |
| Objects appearing or vanishing (all episodes) | Morphing | **No AI motion.** Crossfade between an "empty" and a "with object" still plus a glow overlay in Remotion: 0 credits |
| Memory-objects dissolving (Ep4, 6) | Morphing | Same compositing trick |
| Two copies merging (Ep3 watch) | Hand and object errors | Close insert of an open palm with simple shapes; composite the merge |
| Stair collapse (Ep3) | Debris simulation | Sound, a dust plume in a doorway, and an aftermath still. No collapse shown |
| The clock jumping a minute (Ep3) | Hands and numerals | The clock face uses **baton markers, no numerals**; a single slow insert |
| Ledger, photo, notebook | AI text artifacts | No legible text, ever: red thread, sepia faces, tally marks. Any needed text is a Remotion overlay |
| Lip-sync dialogue | Cost and failure rate | Narration-led. Dialogue is played over profiles, over-shoulders, wides, and backs of heads. At most 6 true lip-sync close-ups per episode |
| Hands on small props | Finger errors | Object inserts on surfaces; simple grips; no fiddly manipulation |
| The crowd at the last train (Ep6) | Many characters | There is no crowd: an almost-empty station, with the crew implied by sound |
| Red lantern raised against the dusk (Ep6) | Hero shot | Silhouettes against the sky with a single red light, which AI handles reliably |
| The cat (optional) | Animal motion | Cut it if it hurts consistency; no story beat depends on it beyond K11 |

### Recommended low-cost proof-of-work (needs a separate spend approval)

A **45-second "Midnight at the Early Shelf" test.** It doubles as the Ep1 cold open and a channel trailer, so the
spend isn't wasted.

| Shot | Content | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Night platform with the clock (location reference) | Location look |
| 2 | Minute hand reaches twelve (insert clip) | Clock motion |
| 3 | Office at night with the empty Early Shelf (still) | Interior lighting |
| 4 | The yellow umbrella glows in (composite, no credits) | Effect test |
| 5–6 | Wren peeks around the doorway; her close-up reaction (two clips) | **Character consistency across two angles** |
| 7 | Ida's silhouette in the doorway (clip) | Second character |
| Audio | Two lines of Wren narration | Voice quality |

The test measures the real credit cost per keyframe and per clip, the retry rate, consistency, and voice. Then the
per-episode budget can be estimated as:
(keyframes × image cost) + (clips × clip cost × retry factor) + audio.

**Credit cap: not yet set by the owner.** Nothing will be spent until it is. It would also be best to run this
after Prompts 02–03 have locked Wren's and Ida's designs.
