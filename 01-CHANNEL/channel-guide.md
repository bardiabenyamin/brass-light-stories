## 1. Specified by the prompt book (treated as fixed)

| Area | Specification |
|---|---|
| Product | Original animated story episodes for YouTube |
| Episode format | About 5 minutes; 16:9, 1920×1080, 30 fps, MP4/H.264, 48 kHz audio |
| Words | About 650–750 spoken words when appropriate; never padded |
| Shorts | 2–3 per episode; 9:16, 1080×1920, 20–45 s; each must work for a viewer who has not seen the episode |
| Visual direction | Original cinematic stylized 3D animation |
| Series model | Series-first: six-episode connected roadmap approved before paid production. Serialized with paired cliffhangers is the default; a standalone anthology is allowed if justified |
| Story rules | Every serialized episode pays off the previous cliffhanger early and delivers a real episode-level payoff. No vague "something strange happened" endings, arbitrary cut-offs, fake deaths, dream resets, or twists without setup |
| Originality | No imitation of named studios, artists, franchises, or films. No real-actor voice imitation. No copyrighted lookalikes. Commercial-use rights verified for all voices and music |
| Publishing ethics | Titles that use curiosity without misleading clickbait. No invented performance claims, links, release dates, or statistics |
| Budget posture | Small AI production budget: reuse characters and locations, simplify risky shots, and run a 30–60 s proof-of-work before scaling |

## 2. Not specified: proposed identity

### 2.1 Option A, a serialized speculative mystery: **APPROVED 2026-11-09**

| Area | Proposal | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | General audience, **13+** (teens and adults). Set as **not "made for kids"** on YouTube | Made-for-kids videos lose end screens, cards, comments, and the notification bell. A cliffhanger-driven series depends on those to bring viewers to the next episode |
| Genre | Serialized **speculative mystery-adventure**: a grounded small world with one impossible element and a season-long mystery | Mysteries generate clue/payoff pairs and earned cliffhangers naturally, which suits the series-first gate |
| Tone | Wonder with an undercurrent of unease. Warm, earnest characters. Light humor that never undercuts stakes. Mild peril | Broad appeal; keeps emotional payoffs sincere |
| Content rating | Roughly **PG** | See boundaries in §3 |
| Cast scale | 2–3 core recurring characters plus at most 1–2 supporting characters per episode | Character consistency is the biggest cost and risk in AI animation, so a small reused cast lowers both |
| Locations | 3–4 recurring core locations plus at most 1 new location per episode | Reuse reduces the number of reference images |
| Storytelling mode | Narration-led with selective on-screen dialogue | Reduces lip-sync shots, which are expensive and fail often, without losing character voice |
| Language | English | No other language indicated |

The actual premise, world, and cast are **not** decided here. Prompt 01 develops them as concept options inside this
identity.

### 2.2 Alternatives (for the owner to compare)

| Option | Identity | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| **B. Cozy fantasy adventure (family, 8+)** | Gentle quests, low peril, a warm found-family cast | Wider age reach. If designated made-for-kids, the channel loses end screens, comments, and notifications. Cliffhangers must stay gentle, so serial pull is weaker |
| **C. Standalone anthology of "one impossible thing" fables (13+)** | A different story each episode, tied together by a recurring narrator or host and a shared theme | Episodes can be watched in any order and continuity risk is low. Return-viewer pull is weaker. **Costs more per episode**, because new characters and locations need new references every time |

## 3. Content boundaries (**APPROVED with Option A, 2026-11-09**; anything excluded below still needs owner approval)

**Allowed:** mild peril; suspense; mystery; loss and grief handled with care; non-graphic conflict; mild
non-graphic injury; mild language at most ("darn", "heck").

**Excluded unless the owner approves first (sensitive content):**

- Graphic violence, gore, or body horror; on-screen death of a main character
- Sexual content or romance beyond innocent affection
- Self-harm, suicide, eating disorders, or substance use
- Real-world politics, religion, or tragedies; real people or real brands
- Hate, slurs, or demeaning stereotypes
- Dangerous imitable acts presented without consequence
- Jump-scare horror content
